A multi-country study published in 2026 examined the relationship between screen time and mental health among 12,500 adolescents aged 13 to 18. Researchers collected data through smartphone usage analytics, sleep surveys, and psychological assessments over a one-year period.

Adolescents reporting more than five hours of recreational screen time daily were more likely to experience symptoms associated with anxiety, sleep disruption, and social withdrawal. However, the study found that educational screen usage showed weaker associations with negative mental health outcomes.

Participants who engaged in regular physical activity and maintained strong offline social relationships demonstrated greater resilience despite higher digital media usage. Researchers emphasized that the quality and context of screen engagement appeared to matter more than total screen exposure alone.

The study recommended promoting balanced technology habits, encouraging physical activity, and improving digital wellness education in schools.
